A productive visit to the quarry begins with a simple plan: choose a team, pick a suitable map, and leave enough room for recovery. Quarry Field Guide brings those decisions together in an approachable reading path. It explains the relationship between miners, shared equipment, and the resources supporting your next run.

This site uses an original idle mining example called Quarry Field. It is a demonstration world, not a verified account of Bomb Farm or any other released game. The advice below explains a coherent sample loop without claiming real reward values, release dates, or developer rules. Treat the scenarios as a place to practise making decisions, rather than a promise of a particular result.

## What these guides cover

The field guide follows a session from preparation to recovery. It introduces the main screens, explains how a three-team rotation can work, and connects map choice to the strength of the miners you actually send. The starting article, <GuideLink slug="guides/how-to-play">How to Play</GuideLink>, puts those pieces into a complete first-session walkthrough.

The walkthrough helps you answer these practical questions about a session:

- Can the active team complete its current route without an emergency change?
- Does every miner have equipment suited to the role you expect it to fill?
- Are your talent choices supporting the same plan as your equipment?
- Can a recovering team rest while another team takes the next run?
- Do you understand which resources your next upgrade will consume?
- Will the harder map fit the time you want to spend in this session?

Equipment, talents, and progression appear as planning topics throughout that walkthrough. They help explain why a balanced team may be more useful than one impressive miner, and why a small upgrade should have a clear purpose. Separate pages for these topics are not available yet. Use the examples to develop a routine and vocabulary for your notes.

## Before increasing difficulty

Moving to a harder map is a decision about consistency. A team that finishes a familiar route comfortably gives you a useful baseline; a team that struggles everywhere makes upgrades difficult to judge.

You do not need a perfect answer to every question. The point is to identify the uncertain part before spending resources. Change one variable, repeat a comparable run, and see whether the change solves the problem you named.

## How to use this field guide

Read for the decision in front of you. If you are unsure where to click, start with the screen overview. If a session keeps ending because your roster is exhausted, return to the recovery explanation. If progress feels uneven, compare your active team's equipment and talents before assuming that the map is the only problem.

Keep a small record of the map, the team, and the reason for each change. A note such as “second team needed more recovery after this route” is more useful than a long list of unexplained results. The guide supplies a structure for those observations; your repeated sessions supply the evidence. Return to the question your notes left open.

## A practical reading order

The following sequence keeps the basic loop visible while you learn the supporting systems. Each step builds on the previous one, so you can pause after one useful lesson.

1. Read <GuideLink slug="guides/how-to-play">How to Play</GuideLink> and identify the eight example screens.
2. Practise preparing and completing one run on a familiar map.
3. Divide the roster into three teams with understandable roles.
4. Observe energy use and recovery across a short rotation.
5. Review equipment and talents for the team that needs the most help.
6. Compare the result before considering a more demanding map.

Return to the earlier step whenever a later decision becomes confusing. A clear first run is a better foundation than a complicated plan you cannot repeat.

## Useful tools

You can do the essential planning with a short session log and a simple team checklist. Record what you changed, what you expected, and what actually happened. Keep equipment assignments visible so that a resting miner does not accidentally hold an item your next team needs.

No calculator, map database, or reward tracker is published on this example site. Begin with the available walkthrough, use ordinary notes for comparisons, and revisit the <GuideLink slug="guides">guide overview</GuideLink> when you need the reading order again.
