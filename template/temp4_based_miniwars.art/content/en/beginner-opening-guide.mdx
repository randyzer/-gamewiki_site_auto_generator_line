## Welcome to the frontier

A new settlement starts with a working crew, a small production plot, and several directions to grow. In the Frontier Ops concept, buying every available structure can leave the crew spread too thin while the border stays exposed. A reliable opening gives each purchase a clear purpose.

This original briefing follows the first ten minutes of a fictional sandbox round. Build a connected supply loop, shelter the workforce, and keep the next response affordable. The setting illustrates this template; it does not describe a released game.

## The rule that protects your opening

> **Prepare a Builder Lodge before adding a second production line.**

Construction capacity supports every later choice. A field needs workers, housing needs a safe location, and finished goods need a short collection route. If the crew is already busy, another building can add cost without helping the settlement. Solve the current bottleneck before creating a new one, and keep a small reserve for an unexpected response at the border.

Let the whole supply chain determine the next purchase.

## Your first ten minutes — step by step

Follow this illustrative sequence, then check whether the map supports your next choice. These are planning examples for the fictional setting.

1. **Establish the starter field.** Give the crew one job and a short collection route.
2. **Prepare the Builder Lodge.** Make room in the work queue before adding another project.
3. **Keep a small reserve.** Leave the next useful response affordable instead of spending everything.
4. **Add a second income source.** Expand from a supply loop that already works.
5. **Shelter the housing zone.** Put workers behind the approach you can protect.
6. **Connect the Supply Depot.** Collect around the district instead of scattered corners.
7. **Replace the weakest field.** Free a useful plot for the next production upgrade.
8. **Introduce a timber workshop.** Begin industry with enough workers to operate it.
9. **Place one Watch Post.** Cover the exposed approach before defending every direction.
10. **Review the next bottleneck.** Check crew capacity, collection distance, and the reserve.

By the end of the opening, look for a compact working district, protected housing, one useful defensive position, and an affordable next response. Those signs matter more than building count. A smaller settlement with spare capacity leaves you more options than a crowded one waiting for workers.

## Three habits worth practising every round

An opening order helps once. These habits also work when the map changes or the first plan runs into trouble. Check them before each purchase.

### 1. Replace weak plots rather than collecting them

Space is a resource. Keeping every starter field can block the industrial district you want to create. Compare the old plot with its replacement, remove structures that no longer serve the plan, and reconnect the cluster before expanding farther away. The useful question is whether an upgrade improves the whole supply chain enough to justify the cost and crew time.

### 2. Read the supply route before buying output

Production means little if collection cannot keep up. Check where goods travel, where workers wait, and whether the depot reaches the district you are actually building. Fix a long route with a better cluster before adding another factory. Keep a reserve while testing the change, so a failed layout does not force you to abandon the border just to reconnect production.

### 3. Keep housing behind the working frontier

Workers support the entire opening. Give their housing a sheltered position and a clear route toward the district it serves. Scattered homes add more places to defend without necessarily adding useful capacity. Aim for a manageable shape: a connected production cluster, a protected housing zone, and defenses facing the approach that matters. Leave room for the next upgrade.

## What to avoid in your first round

- **Do not commit to an oversized army.** A force that consumes every reserve leaves no resources to replace the infrastructure behind it. Prepare a supply line before planning a large deployment.
- **Do not defend every direction equally.** Read the approaches and place the first watch post where it protects a useful route. More defenses are only helpful if they cover a real risk.
- **Do not expand before connecting the core.** Each new border brings more collection, housing, and protection work. Finish the original supply loop before asking the crew to support another district.

## When the settlement is ready to grow

Start the next phase when goods reach the depot, workers have a sheltered home, and the reserve can cover a response. Consider a power station or research hub only when it removes a real limit. An impressive name is not a reason to spend.

If the border becomes active, pause expansion and review the exposed approach. An aerial outpost is a possible mid-game concept, but it still needs reliable supplies and replacement resources. Let the map determine the timing.

## One final check before leaving base

A useful opening leaves choices available. Review the crew, collection route, housing, and reserve before the next purchase. Strengthen whichever part is fragile first.
