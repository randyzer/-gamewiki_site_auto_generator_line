## Who is Hollow Signal?

Hollow Signal is imagined as a small studio exploring the unsettling side of ordinary places. A diner after the last customer leaves, a cinema between screenings and a railway platform after closing time each become a complete stage for a short mystery. Its fictional work emphasizes observation, dialogue and the moment a familiar routine feels wrong.

The Blackwater City concept starts with a missing hour in an emergency log. Detective Mara Vale follows a series of anonymous recordings while other characters finish their overnight shifts. Each viewpoint adds a piece of the timeline. The player does not need to master a complex combat system; the challenge is to notice the difference between what someone remembers and what the evidence shows.

## The four ideas before Midnight Evidence

The imagined back catalog shows how different locations can support the same observation-led approach:

1. **Dead Air** — a radio-room mystery built around a broadcast that returns after the station closes.
2. **The Last Screening** — an empty cinema, a mislabeled reel and an audience nobody remembers.
3. **Room Seven** — a night clerk follows calls from a hotel room removed from the floor plan.
4. **Platform Zero** — a railway worker finds a maintenance report for a platform that no longer appears on the map.

These are original story concepts, not released products. Midnight Evidence combines their strongest threads into one city: interrupted broadcasts, misleading records, night-shift routines and spaces that hold an older version of events. The [gameplay guide](/guides#gameplay) explains how these ideas become the archive's investigation loop.

## The publishing partner: Lantern House

Lantern House is a fictional publishing partner for short narrative adventures. In the demonstration brief, its role is to make a small project's scope understandable: which locations are included, how long each chapter should feel, what the player learns in the opening and where story spoilers belong in public material.

That distinction matters when reading a game guide. A studio shapes the creative work; a publisher can help with release planning, quality checks and the presentation of that work. One does not replace the other, and neither name should be treated as proof that a game is currently available to buy. ## A small ensemble, several perspectives

The production outline uses a restrained cast rather than a large collection of interchangeable characters. Mara Vale leads the investigation. Ellis Reed checks the precinct records. Nina Hart remembers the diner through orders and receipts, Cass Rowan through screening schedules, and Owen Park through maintenance rounds.

Their scenes use different kinds of evidence. A detective compares statements, a clerk notices a broken routine, and a projectionist recognizes a reel in the wrong place. By moving between these perspectives, the story can return to one location without simply repeating the same scene. The [cast notes](/guides#voice-cast) describe these roles as fictional performance directions. They do not attribute voices to real performers or imply a collaboration that has not happened.
