<Callout title="Quick Answer">

Choose a first table, a spending rule and an item carrier before entering. Name the timer watcher and announce larger bets. When the target is covered, pause together and protect the shared balance.

</Callout>

<Section id="section-1" title="The Core Loop">

Use the same team routine each day: prepare, visit, review. The clock makes preparation valuable, and each player’s decisions change the options available to the whole group.

<Step number="1" title="Prepare in the Lobby">

Read the balance and available tools. Check that everyone knows the next objective before the timer starts. A brief conversation helps the group avoid spending its opening minutes resolving a disagreement.

</Step>
<Step number="2" title="Choose an Item Plan">

Pick a tool with a clear purpose and name its carrier. Agree on the supported table and activation cue. One coordinated purchase is easier to use well than a bag of random items.

</Step>
<Step number="3" title="Leave as a Group">

Check that the carrier has collected the tool, then confirm the first table and timer watcher. Leaving with one shared plan prevents the visit from starting with a search for missing teammates.

</Step>
<Step number="4" title="Test the First Table">

Choose a table someone can explain and keep the first test small. Discuss its controls and payout before increasing the stake. Make sure the group understands how each wager affects the common balance.

</Step>
<Step number="5" title="Make an Announced Push">

Announce the stake and item timing before a larger play. Ask teammates to pause side bets while it resolves. This gives everyone time to catch a misunderstanding before the team commits its money.

</Step>
<Step number="6" title="Pause at the Target">

Read the balance against the objective and announce when it is covered. Decide together whether another bet serves the plan. Protecting a useful result deserves the same coordination as earning it.

</Step>
<Step number="7" title="Review Before the Next Day">

Review which decisions changed the balance. Check whether the item arrived, the table was understood and larger bets were announced. Adjust the next plan together instead of chasing a personal comeback.

</Step>

<Section id="section-2" title="Understanding the Quota">

The quota gives each visit its purpose. Read the live target and remaining time instead of relying on remembered numbers. A useful balance only helps when the group can preserve it until the objective is secured.

<Grid>
<Quota label="Day Timer" value="A limited visit">

Pick the first table before entering so the clock is spent playing, not finding the team.

</Quota>
<Quota label="Current Target" value="Read the live objective">

Use the value shown in your run; progression and updates can change assumptions.

</Quota>
<Quota label="Extra Profit" value="A useful buffer">

</Quota>
<Quota label="Missed Objective" value="A recovery decision">

</Quota>

<Section id="section-3" title="Team Coordination">

A shared bank creates a simple problem: one person can spend money another person just earned. Divide the responsibilities before entering so the team knows who is making a decision, who is holding a tool, and who will call for a pause.

<Panel variant="roles" title="Suggested Responsibilities for a Larger Group">

**Caller** Reads the target and announces when the team should pause.

**Item Carrier** Collects the chosen tool and waits for the agreed activation cue.

**Table Lead** Explains the game and places the main coordinated bet.

**Timer Watcher** Announces time remaining before the group needs a recovery plan.

**Other Players** Use agreed small plays or wait while the main bet is being resolved.

</Panel>
<Callout tone="red" title="A rule worth saying out loud">

Announce a large bet before placing it, and leave time for the team to respond.

</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="section-5" title="Tickets & Items: What to Plan First">

Tickets create choices between a useful tool now and flexibility later. Discuss the purpose of a purchase before spending. The notes below are planning prompts for familiar item types; confirm the exact cost, range and effect in the current item description.

<Grid>
<Figure src="/images/cosmetics.svg" caption="Original cosmetic-room diagram: separate appearance shopping from your team’s item budget." />
<Figure src="/images/challenges.svg" caption="Original challenge-board diagram: read the objective before committing tickets to a particular route." />
</Grid>
<Callout title="A purchase discussion for beginners">

1. A tool that supports the first table
2. A way to protect a planned larger attempt
3. A recovery option the group knows how to use
4. A named carrier who can explain the activation
5. A reserve for the next day’s needs
6. Cosmetics in a separate low-pressure session
7. Random purchases only after essentials are covered

</Callout>
<Panel variant="item" title="Loss Protection">

Check which nearby bets the protection covers.

Plan: Gather the covered players before activating the effect.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="A Free Large Attempt">

Read the tool’s stake and activation limits.

Plan: Use a familiar table and agree on the stake.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Rewind Tool">

Check what the reset will restore.

Plan: Name the mistake the group wants to recover from.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Maximum-Bet Increase">

A larger limit also permits a larger loss.

Plan: Discuss the larger amount at risk before using it.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Profit Increase">

Check the duration and eligible profit.

Plan: Activate near the planned attempt, after everyone is ready.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Insurance or Loss Reduction">

Read the covered loss and insurance limit.

Plan: Keep a reserve even when part of a loss is covered.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Control-Affecting Buff">

Consider how the buff changes the controls.

Plan: Choose a carrier who understands the benefit and controls.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Last-Loss Recovery">

Check which previous loss can be recovered.

Plan: Confirm that the tool still targets the intended loss.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Ticket Reward Tool">

Read the qualifying action and reward duration.

Plan: Farm rewards only when the group can afford the risk.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Random Item Purchase">

Random tools may not support your plan.

Plan: Cover the essentials before making an unpredictable purchase.

</Panel>
<Panel variant="item" title="Emergency Body-Part Tool">

Understand the lasting cost of an emergency trade.

Plan: Explain the consequence and agree with the affected player.

</Panel>
<Callout tone="blue" title="Before the Group Leaves">

1. Choose the item for a named purpose
2. Check the current description and cost
3. Complete the purchase together
4. Confirm that the item can be collected
5. Name the player who physically carries it
6. Agree on a clear activation cue

</Callout>
<Grid>
<Panel title="Keep tickets flexible" tone="green">

A shopping plan should leave room for the next objective. Separate cosmetic browsing from a focused run, and ask whether an item solves a real problem for the current group. An unused tool is not helpful simply because it sounded strong in another player’s story.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Review the challenge first" tone="gold">

Read the challenge before preparing a table-specific item plan. If the objective conflicts with the group’s skills, compare the available options in the current game rather than buying a tool and hoping the route will become suitable afterward.

</Panel>
</Grid>

<Section id="section-6" title="Body Parts and Emergency Decisions">

The game’s body-part mechanics turn a funny moment into a decision with lasting consequences. Before an emergency trade, explain what the affected player might lose in communication, vision or movement. Recovery is useful only if the group understands the next-day cost.

<Figure src="/images/recovery.svg" caption="Original recovery diagram: discuss the cost with the affected player before an emergency trade." />
<Grid>
<Panel tone="green" title="Consider an emergency trade when:">

- The team understands the recovery value
- Ordinary options have been discussed
- The affected player agrees to the consequence
- The plan accounts for the following day
- The trade serves a shared objective

</Panel>
<Panel tone="red" title="Pause the decision when:">

- The group is acting without a plan
- The player needs the affected ability next day
- The consequence has not been explained
- A more suitable item option is available

</Panel>
</Grid>
</Section>

<Section id="section-7" title="Solo vs Co-op">



<Grid columns="3">
<Panel title="Solo" tone="gold">

One player makes the decisions and bears every recovery cost.

Use a clear reserve and learn unfamiliar tables carefully. With no teammate to call a pause, decide your stopping point before the next attempt.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Two or Three Players" tone="gold">

A small group can keep the plan visible without too many simultaneous bets.

Assign a main bettor and an item carrier. Share the timer role and announce when the balance has reached the current target.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Four to Six Players" tone="gold">

More teammates create more opportunities to spend the same money at once.

Keep the responsibilities explicit. During a large play, other players should know whether to wait or continue with agreed small bets.

</Panel>
</Grid>
</Section>

<Section id="section-8" title="Common Beginner Mistakes">



<Panel title="Large Bets at an Unfamiliar Table" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** The group may commit its money before understanding the controls or payout.

**Try instead:** Test once with a small stake and have someone explain the result.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Several Unannounced Bets at Once" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** The balance can change faster than the caller can explain what happened.

**Try instead:** Pause side bets while the planned larger action is resolved.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Spending Tickets Immediately" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** A random purchase can remove the flexibility needed for a useful tool later.

**Try instead:** Agree on the item’s purpose, carrier and timing before buying.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Mixing Shopping and a Serious Run" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** The team may disagree about whether tickets are for outfits or survival tools.

**Try instead:** Choose a separate session for browsing cosmetic options.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Accepting a Challenge Without Reading It" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** A table-specific objective can conflict with the route the group already chose.

**Try instead:** Read the full requirement and review the plan before leaving.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Using a Recovery Tool Without a Cue" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** Teammates can act at the wrong time and miss the intended protection.

**Try instead:** Announce what the tool is meant to fix and wait for agreement.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Ignoring a Lasting Penalty" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** A short-term recovery may leave a player struggling in the next visit.

**Try instead:** Discuss communication, vision and movement costs before the trade.

</Panel>
<Panel title="Chasing a Personal Comeback" tone="red">

**Why it hurts:** A player’s attempt to repair their own result can put the team balance at risk.

**Try instead:** Use the result to adjust roles, and keep the next objective collective.

</Panel>
</Section>

<Section id="section-9" title="FAQ">



<FaqItem question="What is the first thing a new team should learn?">

Learn how the shared bank affects everyone. Agree on a spending rule before exploring more complicated tables.

</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="How do we know the current quota?">

Read the objective shown in your current run. Do not rely on an old guide’s exact value without checking the game.

</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="What if the group misses the objective?">

Review the game’s current recovery choices and their costs. Use a plan everyone understands rather than a panic action.

</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="Does every player use the same money?">

Shared-bank play means one player’s spending affects the others. Treat a larger bet as a team decision.

</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="How should a solo player adapt the plan?">

Set the reserve and stopping point before the visit. Take time to learn a table because no teammate can intervene for you.

</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="Which item should we buy first?">

Choose the tool that supports the agreed first table and that a named player knows how to use. Check its current description.

</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="When should we use an emergency trade?">

When the affected player understands and agrees to the cost, and the group has compared its other recovery options.

</FaqItem>

No. They are original template artwork and diagrams. Their captions identify them as illustrations rather than gameplay evidence.
